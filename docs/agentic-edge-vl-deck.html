BLUF · perception is the NPU's half of the agent

Turn up the resolution and the agent becomes the NPU's job.

The LLM loop is decode/bandwidth-bound with the NPU idle. A vision front-end at real resolution is 86% prefill — compute-bound, exactly where the accelerator earns its area. Measured on a real 24/7 camera corpus. This is the bookend to the LLM deck.

01 the flip

Resolution moves the budget

Prefill share runs 21% → 86% from 0.25 MP to native; decode stays flat. Perception at real res is a prefill/TOPS workload, not a decode one. measured

02 the value

Complementary, not a winner

The VLA is precise (96.7% on flagged frames) and sees the still-animal case a motion detector can't — but its recall is the weak leg (4.1× loss). sourced

03 the honesty

Judged against the human ceiling

Species 66.7% vs a 74.8% intra-rater ceiling (≈89% of human repeatability). The costly error is a skunk called a dove, not a dove/cardinal mixup. sourced

04 the design call

Two budgets, one SoC

Hybrid: cheap CV as the recall gate, the VLA for species/direction/stillness. Budget NPU TOPS + bandwidth for the perception front-end; the loop is light. derived

02 / 06

the benchmark · real corpus, free baseline

A camera that actually has to find a skunk.

Qwen2.5-VL-7B on a real backyard camera: 377 clips, 8 nights, day-color and night-IR, cluttered. Not a synthetic set — the hard cases (IR, occlusion, a hand in frame) are naturally distributed.

  • Free baseline on identical inputs: the classical-CV motion pipeline already scores every frame — so the question is "does a 7B VLA beat 200 lines of OpenCV at OpenCV's own job."
  • Human-confirmed labels with an intra-rater ceiling measured, anchoring-controlled.
the a/b, in vision

CV motion detector = today's tech: fires on movement, blind to a still animal, can't name a species or a direction.

VLA = the agentic candidate: perceives, classifies, localizes, reasons — at a prefill/compute cost that scales with pixels.

Decode is resolution-flat (~170 t/s — it's the same short text generation); prefill grows with image tokens and comes to dominate. So the LLM loop is bandwidth/decode (NPU idle), but perception at real resolution is a compute/prefill workload — the accelerator's half of the agent. Resolution is the dial between the two budgets. measured (5090, Q8_0; aggregation derived; contention window excluded).

05 / 06

the failure that matters · and the shape of the answer

A skunk called a dove is the only error that costs.

Species errors split evenly — bird→mammal (49%) ties bird→another-bird (46%). But the asymmetry is in consequence, not frequency:

  • A dove/cardinal mixup costs nothing.
  • 6 raccoons and 1 skunk called "dove" — at night, in IR, on a camera whose whole job is finding skunks. That's the failure a product owner cares about.

Report both rates; weight the decision on the mammal confusions.

the answer is a hybrid

CV gates, VLA judges.

Don't put a 7B VLA alone behind the camera. Use cheap classical CV as the recall gate (it's fast and catches motion), and the VLA for what motion structurally can't do — species, direction, and the still-animal case.

More lopsided in the hybrid's favor than predicted at the start.

06 / 06

the design input · and what's next

Size the perception front-end, then the loop.

The LLM deck said budget DDR + CPU for the generation loop. This adds the other half: the perception front-end is a prefill/compute workload whose cost scales with input resolution — that's where the NPU's TOPS actually go.

design input

Resolution is a first-class axis

Prefill cost (and the NPU budget) is set by input pixels, not model size. Pick the resolution the task needs; it moves 4× the token load and the whole prefill/decode balance.

product rule

Hybrid by default

CV recall-gate + VLA judge. A 7B VLA alone has a recall problem; pairing it with cheap motion detection is both cheaper and better.
